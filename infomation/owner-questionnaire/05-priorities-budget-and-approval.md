# Form 5 — Priorities, budget, launch, and approval

## Form title

Your clothing store — what we should build first

## Form description to paste

This final form helps turn the ideas into a practical launch scope. A feature being interesting does not automatically make it essential. Be direct about priorities, deadlines, budget, and what you want us to recommend. These are planning answers; estimates, responsibilities, and final acceptance criteria will be confirmed after review.

## Builder notes

R = required; O = optional. Keep priority grids small, with one choice per feature and a Not sure column. If grids are uncomfortable on phones, replace each row with a multiple-choice question. Do not use ranking instructions with checkboxes; ordered priorities are collected in paragraph questions.

## Section A — Define the first release

### L01. Which five outcomes or capabilities are non-negotiable for launch?
Paragraph · R. Help: Put them in order. Describe outcomes clearly, such as customers can choose an available size confidently, rather than everything must be premium.

### L02. Which requested features could wait until a later release?
Paragraph · R. Help: Be specific. Write Recommend after reviewing my answers if you want help deciding.

### L03. Which features do you definitely not want?
Paragraph · R. Help: Include layout/animation patterns, marketing tactics, customer features, or integrations you want excluded. Write None yet if appropriate.

### L04. How should we prioritize these discovery features?
Multiple-choice grid · R. Columns: Launch essential; Nice for launch; Later; Do not want; Recommend. Rows: Rich desktop menu; Predictive search; Size/color filters; Quick add; Model/product-photo switch; Multiple collection layouts.

### L05. How should we prioritize these product and cart features?
Multiple-choice grid · R. Columns: Launch essential; Nice for launch; Later; Do not want; Recommend. Rows: Structured size guide; Product zoom/video; Related color-product links; Sticky phone purchase bar; Cart drawer; Cart recommendations.

### L06. How should we prioritize these storytelling and retention features?
Multiple-choice grid · R. Columns: Launch essential; Nice for launch; Later; Do not want; Recommend. Rows: Campaign video; Animated ticker; Lookbook/outfit inspiration; Reels rail; Wishlist; Membership/loyalty integration.

### L07. If time or budget is limited, what should be reduced first?
Multiple choice · R. Choices: Decorative motion; Optional content sections; Optional customer features; Number of visual variations/presets; Number of integrations; Discuss a smaller launch scope; Other. Help: Correct shopping, basic accessibility, mobile usability, and agreed editor behavior remain quality requirements.

### L08. Which areas should receive the most custom design attention?
Checkboxes · R. Choices: Header/navigation; Homepage campaign; Product cards; Collections/filters; Product page; Size guidance; Cart; Brand storytelling; Phone interactions; Editor experience; Other. Help: Choose up to three.

## Section B — Budget and costs

### L09. What currency should we use when discussing the project budget?
Short answer · R.

### L10. What budget range have you set aside for design and development?
Short answer · R. Help: A range is useful. Write Need an estimate if you have not set one. Separate this from stock, photography, marketing, and ongoing Shopify/app fees.

### L11. Is that budget fixed or flexible for a clear business benefit?
Multiple choice · R. Choices: Fixed upper limit; Some flexibility with explanation; Open to phased delivery; Need options at different price levels; Undecided.

### L12. Which work should the project budget include?
Checkboxes · R. Choices: Discovery/design; Theme development; Content entry; Product-data cleanup/import; Photography preparation; Copywriting; Translation; App/integration setup; Migration; Testing; Training; Postlaunch support; Unsure; Other.

### L13. Are there separate budgets for photography, video, fonts, apps, or other services?
Paragraph · O. Help: Include rough ranges/currency and who pays/approves them. No billing details needed.

### L14. What ongoing monthly costs are you comfortable with for optional apps/services?
Short answer · O. Help: Include currency, or write Recommend a minimal-cost setup. Actual service prices will be checked during selection.

### L15. When a feature needs a paid app or extra custom work, how should we decide?
Multiple choice · R. Choices: Recommend the best option with cost/benefit; Prefer native Shopify options first; Prefer fewer apps and simpler features; Present multiple options; Case by case.

## Section C — Timing and readiness

### L16. What is the preferred launch date?
Date · O. Help: Choose your target if known; leave blank if undecided. The next question tells us how fixed it is.

### L17. How fixed is that date?
Multiple choice · R. Choices: Tied to a confirmed event/campaign; Important but movable; Flexible—quality and readiness matter more; Still exploring.

### L18. What event, promotion, or dependency is connected to the launch?
Paragraph · O. Help: Include the event date, any embargo/timezone, and the consequences of missing it.

### L19. What will be ready, and when?
Paragraph · R. Help: Give dates/owners for logo/brand assets, product data, photography, video, size charts, policy text, translations, and store access. Mark unknowns.

### L20. How quickly can the owner/team review designs and answer questions?
Multiple choice · R. Choices: Usually within one working day; Within two/three working days; Weekly review; Availability varies; We need to appoint a reviewer.

### L21. Are there dates when key decision makers will be unavailable?
Paragraph · O. Help: Include timezone and preferred working/review days if relevant.

### L22. If assets or integrations are late, which launch approach do you prefer?
Multiple choice · R. Choices: Move launch until ready; Launch with an agreed smaller scope; Use approved temporary content where suitable; Decide based on the affected feature; Recommend.

## Section D — Review, acceptance, and decision authority

### L23. How much creative direction do you want from us?
Multiple choice · R. Choices: Follow my detailed references closely while creating original work; Recommend a complete direction; Show two focused directions then develop one; Work collaboratively with regular decisions; Other.

### L24. Who will consolidate feedback into one decision?
Short answer · R. Help: This may be the owner or a nominated project lead.

### L25. Who needs to review each area before launch?
Paragraph · R. Help: Brand/design; product/size accuracy; prices/offers; shipping/returns; payments/fulfillment; marketing/tracking; final launch approval.

### L26. What devices can you or your team use to review the actual shopping journey?
Paragraph · R. Help: List available phone/computer models if known, plus any older or common customer devices you can test. Our own test plan will supplement this.

### L27. What specific tasks should you personally be able to complete before approving the store?
Paragraph · R. Help: Examples: find a medium-size shirt, compare fit photos, add the right variant, edit the cart, change the homepage hero in the editor, or create a drop page.

### L28. What must be checked for the theme to count as reusable?
Checkboxes · R. Choices: Change branding without code; Rearrange sections; Use a different product catalog; Create a different visual preset; Install on another store; Use different languages/markets; Preserve content during updates; Developer to recommend a test; Other.

### L29. What should happen when a new idea appears after a design or feature is approved?
Multiple choice · R. Choices: Assess impact and agree scope/time/cost before adding; Save it for a later release unless essential; Discuss case by case; Recommend a clear change process.

### L30. How would you like to review progress?
Checkboxes · R. Choices: Live Shopify preview; Short walkthrough videos; Screenshots of key states; Written decision summaries; Scheduled review calls; Shared task list; Other.

### L31. What would make you reject the first release?
Paragraph · R. Help: Give concrete examples of visual, shopping, phone, content, editor, or operational failures. Avoid all-or-nothing descriptions that cannot be tested.

## Section E — Final briefing and handover

### L32. What should the team be able to do independently after handover?
Paragraph · R. Help: Name the actual tasks and the skill level of the people doing them.

### L33. What level of support do you expect after launch?
Paragraph · O. Help: Bug fixes, content help, campaign changes, app issues, training, or ongoing development. Any support period/service will be agreed separately.

### L34. Which three decisions would you most like us to recommend for you?
Paragraph · R. Help: Examples: visual direction, navigation, product layout, app choices, launch scope, or content requirements.

### L35. Is anything in your earlier answers a preference rather than a firm requirement?
Paragraph · O. Help: Identify the items where we have freedom to propose a better approach.

### L36. Is there any additional requirement, idea, example, or concern we should know before designing?
Paragraph · O. Help: Add links and explain context. If you changed a previous answer, reference its question ID so we can reconcile it.
